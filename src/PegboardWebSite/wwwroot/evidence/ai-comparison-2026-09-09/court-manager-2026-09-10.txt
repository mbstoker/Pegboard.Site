# Court Manager — supplementary single-product evaluation, 2026-09-10

**Why this exists.** All three runs on 2026-09-09 were given `app.court-manager.com`, which is Court
Manager's admin dashboard and serves a login form. All three therefore scored it U on every
criterion and it appears in the published table as three dashes. Its actual product site is
[`court-manager.com`](https://www.court-manager.com/). The error was ours, it was caught in the
adversarial claims review of the `/compare` build, and this run fixes it.

**What it is and is not.** Same 100-point rubric, same club profile, same scoring discipline
(2/1/0/U, never convert U to zero, self-consistency gate, marketing-claim-is-not-a-2), run against
the correct URL. It is **one product on a later date**, not a fourth full evaluation, and it must
not be presented as a column alongside the three runs.

**Result: 72.5 / 100, 15 of 15 criteria resolved, range 72.5–72.5.**

For context only — and the dates differ, so this is orientation rather than a placing — run 2 scored
ePegboard 85.5 and ShuttlePilot 73.0. Court Manager at 72.5 sits level with ShuttlePilot within any
reasonable margin, and it is one of very few products in the whole exercise with nothing left
unresolved.

## The structural finding

**Court Manager is two diverging products under one name**, and it changes how the scores read.
The **iOS app** (£9.99 one-off) was last updated **v1.7.1, August 2023**, and is what the marketing
site's long help section describes. The **hosted web version** at `app.court-manager.com` is on
Release 1.4.4, actively developed through 2026, subscription-billed with a Pro tier, and carries
the capabilities that matter — tournaments, session summary emails, fairness alerts, offline-first
sync. Its feature history is discoverable only through a "What's New" modal on the login page
footer, and **its price is published nowhere.**

## Where it is strong, and it is strong in our area

Six of its eight 2s cluster on live-session mechanics: arrivals, allocation, sit-out fairness,
variety, manual override, resilience. That is the club-night core, and the documentation describes
rules and edge cases rather than benefits — the pick window's default, the stall condition when
players equal courts, what happens to a reused court's players, the limit on the repeat-avoidance
rule.

**It scores 2 on resilience where ePegboard scores 1.** Offline-first with explicit captive-portal
handling is a documented answer to the exact objection our own weakness list concedes.

## Where it is weak

- **Skill balancing is the weakest link in the highest-weighted area.** Ranks are hand-typed, never
  move on their own, and the combining rule is never stated. A committee commits to maintaining a
  manual ladder for 26 people indefinitely. This is the wedge, and it is the same wedge that is open
  against Qcourt, Shuttl and SmashHQ.
- **No per-pick rationale.** Same gap as almost everything else in the field.
- **Fairness and variety both regressed inside the current release line.** The vendor's own
  changelog records losing players slipping out of the pick window, auto-assign repeatedly grouping
  similar players with court time unevenly shared, and players falling off the board entirely — all
  during 2026, all now fixed and disclosed. The evaluator did **not** downgrade for this, flagging it
  as a maturity finding instead, and states plainly that a stricter reading would take criteria 4 and
  5 to 1 and **the total to 63.5**. That is the one place a reasonable evaluator could differ, and it
  is worth nine points.
- **Sole developer**, no company entity beyond a copyright line, single support mailbox, no SLA.

## Caveats on this run

- **Documented, never demonstrated.** No account was created; the running application is entirely
  behind an admin login. Every functional finding rests on the vendor describing its own product.
- **Different date** to the three main runs, and a **single-product run**, so it cannot be read as a
  placing within them.
- **No U anywhere is a property of the source, not generosity** — the marketing page carries an
  unusually long operations manual and the release-notes modal is effectively a public changelog.
