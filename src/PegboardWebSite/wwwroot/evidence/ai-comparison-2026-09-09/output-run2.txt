# Running the club night: 30 products, one rubric

**Independent software evaluation for a UK recreational badminton club**
**Access date for all sources: 9 September 2026**

A weekly social badminton club — 18–26 attendees, four courts, mixed strengths, players
arriving late and leaving early — needs balanced games without unfair waits. Thirty candidate
products were assessed against the club's pre-declared 100-point rubric, using each vendor's own
documentation, retrieved from the canonical URL supplied for it and nowhere else.

**Candidate count: 30.** 29 with a supplied canonical URL, plus SmashHQ with none.
27 scored · 2 unscoreable (no public product content) · 1 UNLOCATED.

---

## Method and scoring key

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **2** | Fully meets the criterion — the source describes the rule, the edge cases and the limits |
| **1** | Partially meets it — capability evidenced, but operational detail absent (a feature bullet or benefit statement is a 1, never a 2) |
| **0** | Verified not to meet it |
| **U** | Insufficient evidence. **Never converted to zero.** |

Each criterion contributes `weight × score ÷ 2`. U contributes nothing to the verified score and
is carried instead as unresolved headroom.

## C. Overall ranking

A defensible ranking **can** be calculated for the top of the table. The four highest-scoring
products resolved 14 or 15 of 15 criteria, and their possible ranges do not overlap:
ePegboard 85.5–85.5, ShuttlePilot 73.0–77.0, Court Buddy 61.0–66.0, Racket Social 59.0–64.0.
Positions 1 and 2 are secure on the evidence.

Below roughly position 12 the ranking is **not** defensible. From CourtsFlow downwards,
unresolved maxima exceed verified scores, and the ordering reflects how much each vendor
published rather than what each product does. Those products are listed in rank order for
completeness, not recommended against. To resolve them would require, at minimum: reachable
documentation of each product's allocation rule, its skill model, and its sit-out policy —
the three criteria most often left as marketing claims.

> **Court Buddy is excluded from the recommendation on sport-fit grounds.** It scores third on
> the rubric, and that score is honest — its fairness and sit-out documentation is genuinely
> good. But every page of court-buddy.com describes a pickleball product: the title, the DUPR
> rating integration, the closing call to action. Badminton is never mentioned. The rubric
> contains no criterion for sport fit, so the score stands; the recommendation cannot. A
> badminton committee should treat it as evidence that this class of product exists, not as a
> candidate.

## D. Top three

### 1. ePegboard — 85.5 / 100, 15 of 15 resolved

The only candidate with a separate documentation site, and the only one that publishes its rating
mathematics in full: `team_rating = (p1 + p2) / 2`,
`expected_share = 1 / (1 + 10^((opponent − team) / 2200))`, actual share taken from the score
ratio rather than a binary win, and `K = max(500 − 10 × rated_games, 30)`. The picker names eight
weighting signals (expected closeness, skill balance, wait fairness, gender balance, avoid recent
repeats, partner/opponent variety, queue fairness, straight-back-on avoidance) and three hard
toggles, shows a par score for expected closeness, and offers a "Why was this game suggested?"
panel that compares the chosen game against the alternatives it rejected. Kiosk mode with PIN
gating and 60-second auto-revert, attendance trends (active / lapsing / dormant), CSV export, and
a free club-night workflow with no card on file complete it.

### 2. ShuttlePilot — 73.0 / 100, 14 of 15 resolved

Ties ePegboard exactly on the four core club-night criteria. It states its generator rule plainly
on one public page — sides close on rating, plus a penalty for pairs and foursomes that have
already played together this evening — and it is the only product that equalises sit-outs
**across sessions**, not just tonight. Late arrivals are handled explicitly: check them in, press
Generate round again, and courts already playing are left alone while empty ones fill. Pairing
preferences (play together, never partnered, never opposed) are documented as strong preferences
rather than hard blocks. Free in beta, phone-first, no seat limits.

### 3. Racket Social — 59.0 / 100, 14 of 15 resolved

Court Buddy outscores it but is pickleball software, so Racket Social is the third product a
badminton committee could actually adopt. It publishes the clearest single fairness rule of any
candidate — the rotation "equalises rest time so nobody sits out twice in a row while others never
get a break" — names its rating library (OpenSkill, with a link to the source repository), works
genuinely offline as a PWA, and offers a public read-only wallboard at `/shared/<code>` via QR or
link with no sign-up. Free, no account required, 23 languages, 6–24 players across 1–6 courts
described as typical and well-tested.

## E. Material weaknesses of the winner

Each of these is the reason a criterion scored 1 rather than 2.

1. **Offline operation is a paid, separate product.** The browser version's behaviour when venue
   Wi-Fi drops is not documented anywhere. Fully offline running requires the Windows desktop app
   at £60 per club per year. For a sports hall with unreliable signal this is the single most
   consequential gap, and it is why criterion 14 scored 1. Racket Social and Badminton Peg System
   both do offline for nothing.
2. **The picker's signal weights are not published.** Eight signals are named; their relative
   strengths are not. A club cannot predict or tune the trade-off between "close game" and "long
   wait", and cannot answer a member who asks why waiting longest did not get them on court. This
   is why criterion 6 scored 1, despite the per-pick explanation panel being the best of any
   candidate.
3. **Payments are a ledger, not a payment system.** The vendor is explicit — "a record, not a card
   machine". Money is collected by cash, cheque or transfer and typed in afterwards. There is no
   partial-payment ledger, no bulk mark-paid button, and each save overwrites the previous entry.
   ShuttleOps and QourtX both move real money through Stripe. Criterion 12 scored 1.
4. **Partner and opponent variety is a soft preference with no stated guarantee.** "Avoid recent
   repeats" and "partner/opponent variety" are two signals among eight, with no documented window
   — no rule of the form "not the same four within N games". Criterion 5 scored 1. ShuttlePilot
   has the same limitation and the same score.
5. **Tournament workflows were not verified in depth.** Handicap tournaments with balanced pairs,
   group stages and live standings are described, and dedicated how-to pages exist, but the
   tournament documentation was not opened during this evaluation. Criterion 13 was capped at 1
   for that reason — an evidence limit, not a product finding.
6. **Phone operation is discouraged.** "Tablet or laptop-sized screens recommended." ShuttlePilot
   and NextFour are explicitly designed for one-handed phone use by an organiser who is also
   playing.
7. **Every score is documented, not demonstrated.** The software was not run. No score in this
   evaluation rests on observed behaviour, independent review or third-party verification — for
   ePegboard or for anything else.

## G. Confidence level and unresolved evidence

### On the primary requirement, first and second place are tied

Criteria 1–4 — check-in and late joiners, dynamic allocation, skill-balanced doubles and sit-out
fairness — carry 40 of the 100 points and are the club's stated primary requirement.

- ePegboard scores 6 + 12 + 12 + 10 = **40**
- ShuttlePilot scores 6 + 12 + 12 + 10 = **40**

The entire 12.5-point gap between them sits in criteria 5–15.

### How much of the leader's margin is evidence availability rather than capability

Breaking the 12.5-point gap down:

| Source of gap | Points |
|---|---:|
| Criterion 8 — ePegboard publishes its rating formula; ShuttlePilot says only "ELO" | 3.5 |
| Criterion 10 — kiosk mode documented vs. an organiser phone interface | 2.5 |
| Criterion 11 — session and attendance reporting documented vs. not | 2.5 |
| Criterion 13 — tournaments described vs. unresolved (U, not weak) | 2.0 |
| Criterion 15 — a committed free price vs. a beta with no post-beta price | 2.0 |
| **Total** | **12.5** |

**Roughly ten of those twelve-and-a-half points are documentation depth on secondary criteria,
not demonstrated superiority on the club night itself.** This is precisely the failure mode the
scoring discipline warns about, and it is visible in the data: ePegboard is the only candidate
that resolved 15 of 15 criteria, and the only one in the field with a standalone documentation
site. Its lead is real under the rubric as written, and it is also partly an artefact of being
the best-documented product among the thirty.

### What would have to be true for ShuttlePilot to overtake

ShuttlePilot's ceiling is 87.5 — above ePegboard's fixed 85.5 — so an overtake is arithmetically
available. It would require, in descending order of leverage:

1. **Publish the ELO parameters** — formula, K schedule, starting values, how doubles ratings
   combine: **+3.5**. The largest single lever and the cheapest to fix.
2. **Resolve competitions**, currently its only U. Any documented tournament or league support:
   up to **+4**.
3. **Commit to a post-beta price** with stated limits: **+2**. "Free while we're in beta" is not
   something a committee can budget against.
4. **Add a shared court display or kiosk mode**: **+2.5**.
5. **Document session and attendance reporting**: **+2.5**. It already tracks sit-outs across
   sessions, so the data exists.

Note the asymmetry: every one of these is a publishing or packaging change, not new matchmaking
capability. Conversely, ePegboard cannot easily close its own gaps — free offline operation, real
payment processing and published picker weights are product decisions, not documentation ones.

### Confidence by band

- **Positions 1–2: high.** Ranges do not overlap; 14–15 of 15 criteria resolved; both assessed
  from detailed vendor documentation.
- **Positions 3–10: moderate.** Ranges are narrow, but several rest on marketing-page feature
  lists rather than operational documentation. Cross-check (f) forced three downward corrections
  in this band.
- **Positions 11 and below: low.** Unresolved maxima exceed verified scores. These positions
  measure disclosure, not product.

### Unresolved evidence

- **ShuttleOps** — FAQ answers and Pulse article bodies would not expand in the rendered page.
  These would have resolved criteria 6 and 8, worth up to 15 points.
- **ShuttleFlow** — the "public ranking ruleset" is referenced but was not found at either
  `/rules` or `/ranking-rules`. Would have resolved criterion 8.
- **ePegboard** — the tournament how-to pages were not opened and `/insights-reports/` returned
  404, capping criterion 13 at 1.
- **Court Manager** — reachable but serves only an admin login wall; nothing resolvable.
- **QSENPAI** — supplied URL returns a hosting 404; nothing resolvable.
- **Not attempted for any candidate:** running the software, contacting any vendor, or reading
  any independent review or app-store rating. **Every score in this report is documented, never
  demonstrated.**
