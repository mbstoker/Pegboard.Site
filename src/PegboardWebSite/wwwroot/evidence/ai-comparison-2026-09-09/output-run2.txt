## C. Overall ranking

A defensible ranking **can** be calculated for the top of the table. The four highest-scoring
products resolved 14 or 15 of 15 criteria and their ranges do not overlap: ePegboard 85.5-85.5,
ShuttlePilot 73.0-77.0, Court Buddy 61.0-66.0, Racket Social 59.0-64.0. **Positions 1 and 2 are
secure on the evidence.**

Below about position 12 the ranking is **not** defensible. From CourtsFlow downwards, unresolved
maxima exceed verified scores, and the ordering reflects how much each vendor published rather
than what each product does.

**Court Buddy is excluded from the recommendation on sport-fit grounds.** It scores third and
that score is honest - its fairness and sit-out documentation is genuinely good. But every page
of court-buddy.com describes a pickleball product: the title, the DUPR rating integration, the
closing call to action. Badminton is never mentioned. The rubric contains no criterion for sport
fit, so the score stands; the recommendation cannot.

## D. Top three

1. **ePegboard - 85.5, 15 of 15 resolved.** The only candidate with a separate documentation
   site, and the only one publishing its rating mathematics in full - team rating, expected share
   with a 2200-point spread constant, a decaying K-factor from 500 to a floor of 30. The picker
   names eight weighting signals and three hard toggles, shows a par score for expected closeness,
   and offers a "Why was this game suggested?" panel comparing the chosen game against the
   alternatives it rejected. Kiosk mode, attendance trends, CSV export, free club-night workflow.
2. **ShuttlePilot - 73.0, 14 of 15 resolved.** Ties ePegboard exactly on the four core club-night
   criteria. States its generator rule plainly on one public page - sides close on rating, a
   penalty for pairs and foursomes already played this evening - and is **the only product that
   equalises sit-outs across sessions**, not just tonight. Late arrivals handled explicitly.
   Free in beta, phone-first, no seat limits.
3. **Racket Social - 59.0, 14 of 15 resolved.** Court Buddy outscores it but is pickleball
   software, so this is the third product a badminton committee could actually adopt. Publishes
   the clearest single fairness rule of any candidate - nobody sits out twice in a row while
   others never rest - names its rating library (OpenSkill) with a source link, works genuinely
   offline as a PWA, offers a public read-only wallboard link. Free, no sign-up.

## E. Material weaknesses of the winner

Each of these is why a criterion scored 1 rather than 2.

1. **Offline operation is a paid, separate product.** The browser version's behaviour when venue
   Wi-Fi drops is not documented anywhere. Fully offline running requires the Windows desktop app
   at GBP 60 per club per year. For a sports hall with unreliable signal this is the single most
   consequential gap, and it is the reason criterion 14 scored 1. Racket Social and Badminton Peg
   System both do offline for nothing.
2. **The picker's signal weights are not published.** Eight signals are named; their relative
   strengths are not. A club cannot predict or tune the trade-off between "close game" and "long
   wait", and cannot answer a member who asks why waiting longest did not get them on court. This
   is why criterion 6 scored 1 despite the per-pick explanation panel being the best of any
   candidate.
3. **Payments are a ledger, not a payment system.** The vendor is explicit - "a record, not a card
   machine". Money is collected by cash, cheque or transfer and typed in afterwards. No
   partial-payment ledger, no bulk mark-paid, and each save overwrites the previous entry.
   ShuttleOps and QourtX both move real money through Stripe. Criterion 12 scored 1.
4. **Partner and opponent variety is a soft preference with no stated guarantee.** "Avoid recent
   repeats" and "partner/opponent variety" are two signals among eight, with no documented window
   - no rule of the form "not the same four within N games". Criterion 5 scored 1. ShuttlePilot
   has the same limitation and the same score.
5. **Tournament workflows were not verified in depth.** Handicap tournaments with balanced pairs,
   group stages and live standings are described and dedicated how-to pages exist, but the
   tournament documentation was not opened during this evaluation. Criterion 13 was capped at 1
   for that reason, not because of anything found.
6. **Phone operation is discouraged.** "Tablet or laptop-sized screens recommended." ShuttlePilot
   and NextFour are explicitly designed for one-handed phone use by an organiser who is also
   playing.
7. **Every score is documented, not demonstrated.** The software was not run. No score in this
   evaluation rests on observed behaviour, independent review or third-party verification - for
   ePegboard or for anything else.

## F. When another product is the better choice

- **The hall has no usable Wi-Fi and there is no budget: Racket Social.** A genuine offline-first
  PWA - match generation, result recording and roster management all keep working with no signal,
  syncing when it returns. Free, no sign-up. The catch is real: sessions live in the browser of
  the device that created them, so a lost or wiped tablet loses the history, and cross-device sync
  is still a roadmap item.
- **Sit-out fairness across weeks matters most: ShuttlePilot.** The only product that counts who
  has sat out least "across every session, not just tonight". For a club where the same two or
  three people always seem to miss out, this is the best-specified fairness rule found anywhere in
  the field.
- **The committee wants subs collected properly: ShuttleOps** - a UK company with published tiers
  (GBP 0 up to 10 members, 4 to 25, 8 to 100, 12 with WhatsApp messaging), Stripe and Direct
  Debit, stated transaction fees and payout timings. **QourtX** if you also want VAT handled at
  checkout and per-partner fee splitting, but note its two published price schemes contradict
  each other.
- **Leagues, tournaments and governing-body links dominate: SportyHQ.** Tournaments, team leagues,
  solo leagues, box leagues, ladders, ranking sanctioning and an API - by far the deepest
  competition feature set in the field. It documents no court-rotation capability at all, so it
  would need pairing with a club-night tool.
- **A published, inspectable rating standard is the priority: Ladderly.** Microsoft TrueSkill,
  explained on its own page: mu and sigma, a displayed skill of mu-3sigma, starting values of 25
  and 8.333, placement matches, reduced rating impact when a game goes to deuce. Only ePegboard
  matches this level of rating transparency.
- **Zero budget, no ratings wanted, up to 200 players: Badminton Peg System.** GBP 0 forever, no
  subscriptions, no per-player fees, works offline after setup, tablet view showing every court
  side by side. No rating system and no documented skill balancing - for a club that just wants
  the pegboard digitised, that may be the point.
- **The club is in the Philippines: PaQueueKa or ShuttleFlow.** Both built around Philippine clubs
  - peso pricing, local season naming, per-head and per-hour fee models. PaQueueKa additionally
  documents a specific partner rule (no back-to-back re-pairing).
- **The club plays pickleball, or wants DUPR: Court Buddy.** Highest rubric score after the two
  leaders, with an explicit sit-out rule and DUPR-submission-ready competition reports - but a
  pickleball product throughout, never mentioning badminton.

## G. Confidence, and how much of the lead is evidence rather than capability

**On the primary requirement, first and second place are tied.** Criteria 1-4 - check-in and late
joiners, dynamic allocation, skill-balanced doubles and sit-out fairness - carry 40 of the 100
points and are the club's stated primary requirement. ePegboard scores 6+12+12+10 = 40.
ShuttlePilot scores 6+12+12+10 = 40. **The entire 12.5-point gap between them sits in criteria
5-15.**

Breaking that gap down: 3.5 points from criterion 8, where ePegboard publishes its rating formula
and ShuttlePilot says only "ELO"; 2.5 from kiosk mode; 2.5 from session reporting; 2 from
tournaments, where ShuttlePilot is unresolved rather than weak; 2 from a committed price against a
beta with no published post-beta price. **Roughly ten of the twelve-and-a-half points are
documentation depth on secondary criteria, not demonstrated superiority on the club night itself.**

This is the failure mode the scoring discipline warns about, and it is visible in the data:
ePegboard is the only candidate that resolved 15 of 15 criteria, and the only one with a
standalone documentation site. Its lead is real under the rubric as written, and it is also partly
an artefact of being the best-documented product in the field.

### What would have to be true for ShuttlePilot to overtake

Its ceiling is 87.5, above ePegboard's fixed 85.5, so an overtake is arithmetically available.

- **Publish the ELO parameters** (formula, K schedule, starting values, how doubles ratings
  combine): +3.5. The largest single lever and the cheapest to fix.
- **Resolve competitions** - currently its only U. Any documented tournament or league support:
  up to +4.
- **Commit to a post-beta price** with stated limits: +2.
- **Add a shared court display or kiosk mode**: +2.5.
- **Document session and attendance reporting**: +2.5. It already tracks sit-outs across sessions,
  so the data exists.

**Note the asymmetry: every one of these is a publishing or packaging change, not new matchmaking
capability.** Conversely, ePegboard cannot easily close its own gaps - free offline operation, real
payment processing and published picker weights are product decisions, not documentation ones.

### Confidence by band

- **Positions 1-2: high.** Ranges do not overlap; 14-15 of 15 criteria resolved; both assessed
  from detailed vendor documentation.
- **Positions 3-10: moderate.** Ranges are narrow but several rest on marketing-page feature
  lists. Cross-check (f) forced three downward corrections in this band.
- **Positions 11 and below: low.** Unresolved maxima exceed verified scores. These positions
  measure disclosure, not product.
- **Not resolvable from the supplied URLs:** ShuttleOps' FAQ answers and Pulse articles (accordion
  content did not render), which would have resolved its criteria 6 and 8; ShuttleFlow's public
  ranking ruleset (page not found at two plausible paths), which would have resolved criterion 8;
  ePegboard's tournament how-to pages and the Insights and reports index (404), which capped
  criterion 13.
- **Not attempted anywhere:** running any of the software, contacting any vendor, reading any
  independent review or app-store rating.
