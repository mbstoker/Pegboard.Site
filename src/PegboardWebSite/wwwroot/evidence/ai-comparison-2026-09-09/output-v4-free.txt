## D. Top three

### 1. ePegboard — my recommendation

This is the only candidate whose entire rubric is publicly resolved, and it is unusually specific about the actual club-night algorithm.

The picker is documented as considering closeness, skill, waiting time, gender balance, repeat avoidance, partner/opponent variety, queue fairness and straight-back-on avoidance. It can also force inclusion of the longest-waiting player, restrict the queue considered, prevent immediate re-selection, lock a player, swap players and support sticky pairs. — ePegboard Docs

It also explicitly handles arrivals, drop-ins and last-minute changes, gives the reason for a suggested game, records results/history, and has a shared-device kiosk mode. — ePegboard Docs +2

The rating system is documented as team-doubles Elo, including score margin, new-player settling and team-result treatment. — ePegboard Docs

It also covers attendance, reports, payments, competitions and league support. Crucially, its payment documentation admits that it is not a payment processor: it records cash/cheque/bank-transfer payments rather than taking card payments itself. — ePegboard Docs

Pricing is unusually attractive: the club-night workflow is free, while the Windows offline application is £60/year after a 60-day trial. — ePegboard +1

Why it wins: it is not merely feature-rich; the vendor has documented the decision rules behind the most important part of this evaluation.

### 2=. ShuttleFlow

ShuttleFlow is particularly strong for your exact live-night problem. It explicitly describes the difficult case of 20 players, four courts, latecomers and someone leaving early, and says it auto-suggests balanced matches using skill, recency and wait time. It supports manual assignment and synchronised live court grids across phones, tablets and laptops. — ShuttleFlow +1

Its ranking rules are unusually explicit: points, seasons, skill tiers, skill locks, minimum-match conditions and anti-farming rules are published. — ShuttleFlow

Why it isn't #1: the competition/tournament workflow was not sufficiently established, and its pairing explanation is less developed than ePegboard's.

### 2=. Racket Social

Racket Social is arguably the most interesting pure social-rotation product.

It explicitly targets badminton doubles, skill-balanced courts, fair sit-outs, partner/opponent variety, OpenSkill/Elo, public live session views and offline operation. — Racket Social

Its current changelog gives unusually useful implementation evidence: the algorithm was recalibrated around fairness, partner variety and opponent variety; it has an auto-rotate sitting-out option; skill-balanced matchmaking can factor ratings; and tournament mode supports round-robin singles and fixed-pair league doubles. — Racket Social

Why it isn't #1: the current product is much less complete as a club administration platform—payments/member administration is not established—and its sessions historically lived primarily on the creating device, with Club mode still developing. — Racket Social

## E. Material weaknesses of the winner

ePegboard is not perfect.

### D. Top three

Payments are bookkeeping, not payment processing. It records who paid and what they owe; it does not take card/online payments. — ePegboard Docs

The web experience depends on connectivity. The offline option is a separate Windows desktop app; new functionality lands on the web app first. — ePegboard

The main shared display is a desk tablet/laptop. A larger court-facing display is optional rather than the core model. — ePegboard +1

Large/multi-site clubs are a special case. The normal club-night product is free, but the pricing page says very large/multi-site clubs, leagues and one-off competitions should contact the vendor. — ePegboard

The evidence is still vendor documentation. I did not independently operate a real 18–26-player session, so a documented algorithm is not the same thing as a demonstrated one.

## F. When another product would be better

**Choose Racket Social instead**

For a club whose main requirement is simple social doubles rotation, with little interest in payments, membership administration or formal competitions. Its offline PWA, sit-out balancing and variety controls are excellent fits. — Racket Social +1

**Choose ShuttleFlow instead**

For a club where the live queue and changing attendance are overwhelmingly important, particularly if late arrivals/leavers and multi-device operation are frequent. Its documentation directly addresses those scenarios.
