# UK social badminton software evaluation

Research date: **12 September 2026**. **33 candidates** were evaluated from the supplied canonical addresses, with linked official product/help/pricing pages in scope. Club: weekly open/social night, 18–26 attendees, four courts, mixed strengths, late arrivals, early departures and temporary rests.

**Recommendation: trial ePegboard first.** It has the strongest documented combination of live court allocation, selection explanations, manual exceptions, kiosk permissions and reporting. This is an evidence-led shortlist, not a measured claim that its algorithm produces the best games. No product was tested through a real club night or controlled 26-player simulation.

## Method and scoring interpretation

The supplied weights are unchanged. **2 = VERIFIED FULL**, **1 = VERIFIED PARTIAL**, **0 = VERIFIED ABSENT**, **U = UNVERIFIED**. “Verified” here means supported by the official source read, not independently performance-tested. Vendor operational descriptions may support 2; bullets, benefit claims, incomplete components or stated qualifications receive 1. Unknown features remain U. An explicit future feature can support 0 only where the current criterion has no supported component; if informal/partial competition is already supported, it remains 1.

Criteria are interpreted for the stated club. C1 requires a usable live arrival/late-entry procedure. C2 requires ongoing freed-court allocation rather than merely a preplanned fixture. C3 concerns opposing doubles teams, not just a quartet sharing a skill band. C4 covers real waiting/rest fairness with attendance changes, not simply game counts. C5 covers both partners and opponents. C6 rewards reasons for choices as well as visible state; queue displays or public broad factors are partial. C7 includes live manual choices and availability/special-case handling. C8 includes individual skill development: manual grades, win-rate standings and seasonal points are partial. C9 includes live result entry, statistics and persistent history. C10 includes operating a multicourt/shared kiosk with permissions, not merely four illustrated cards. C11 includes attendance and session reports. C12 combines member administration and payments; a roster alone, fee calculator or payment record alone is partial. C13 concerns actual event/league organisation, not merely a leaderboard. C14 combines device accessibility and resilient operation/recovery. C15 requires a usable current tariff and clear practical limits for four courts and the expected roster, including onboarding constraints.

This interpretation intentionally holds related products to the same standard. Unspecified latency, fairness and scale are not inferred from branding, customer counts, testimonials, search visibility or documentation length. App-store developer descriptions count as vendor documentation, not independent app testing. Similarly named alternatives and suggested unrelated apps contribute no evidence. No sport-fit bonus or penalty is applied.

The browser executed JavaScript. Hydration-only initial views were retried. Reading a public live session directory demonstrates that directory, not its claimed scheduling quality. Illustrations, seed-data selectors, FAQs and release notes remain vendor evidence. No account was created, app installed, payment made or real club session altered.

## C–G. Decision, weaknesses, alternatives and confidence

### D. Top three by verified score

1. **ePegboard — 70.5/100**, possible 70.5–70.5; 15/15 resolved.

2. **ShuttlSync — 62/100**, possible 62–66; 14/15 resolved.

3. **ShuttlePilot — 59.5/100**, possible 59.5–63.5; 14/15 resolved.

**ePegboard** is first because the picker has a freed-court/prep loop and a documented reasons panel, while special-request controls explicitly handle players still on court. Kiosk PIN/action permissions and date/profile-filtered reports are described operationally. Its score is not based on the number of pages: the detail changes the evidential status of these particular criteria. [Picker](https://docs.epegboard.com/club-nights/the-picker/), [special requests](https://docs.epegboard.com/club-nights/special-requests/), [kiosk](https://docs.epegboard.com/club-nights/kiosk-mode/), [session reports](https://docs.epegboard.com/insights/session-summary/).

**ShuttlSync** is especially relevant to uneven court finishing: it gives the organiser concrete choices to rotate one court, wait, reshuffle or bring in fresh bench players, and supports live manual rearrangement. Its free two-court tier is insufficient; the stated £9.99/month club Pro tier covers four live courts. Its late-arrival priority needs a fairness trial. [Fair rotation](https://www.shuttlsync.com/features/fair-rotation), [live sessions](https://www.shuttlsync.com/features/live-sessions), [pricing on homepage](https://www.shuttlsync.com/).

**ShuttlePilot** is third because its guide describes mid-round regeneration that leaves active games intact and fills empty courts, late/early attendance handling, manual courts/swaps, and saved set-score/history behaviour. It is a credible live-night pilot candidate, but historical sit-out equalisation is different from attendance-normalised waiting tonight and strength preferences are soft. [Official workflow](https://shuttlepilot.io/).

**ServeLeague**, fourth, has richer operational attendance-change, scoring, membership/payment and league evidence, but its drop-in guide uses generated rounds, similar session scores and lower-rated-player early finishing rather than demonstrating asynchronous skill-balanced doubles across four courts. UK payment-method applicability is also unresolved. [Drop-in reference](https://serveleague.com/tutorials/reference/drop-in-leagues/), [leagues](https://serveleague.com/features/league-management/), [payments](https://serveleague.com/features/payments/).

### E. Winner’s material weaknesses

ePegboard receives **1**, not 2, for skill balancing, fairness and variety. Its picker optimises competing soft signals; hard requirements can force less balanced games. There is no documented guarantee that an extreme mixed-strength or constrained pool will yield close games or identical waits. [Picker rules](https://docs.epegboard.com/club-nights/the-picker/).

Its team Elo uses score margin and confidence but cannot isolate the contribution of an individual doubles player. Ended sessions cannot reopen. Its payment tool records a single amount per player/session, replacing the prior figure; it does not collect online/card payments or provide a payment-event ledger. [Ratings](https://docs.epegboard.com/ratings/overview/), [session lifecycle](https://docs.epegboard.com/club-nights/sessions/), [payments](https://docs.epegboard.com/club-nights/payments/).

Competition support is currently doubles-focused, with singles/team events on the roadmap. Offline Windows is a separate paid edition, and new capabilities arrive on the web first; its existence is not proof that the free web version has identical offline behaviour. Free online pricing is documented, but quantitative web capacity/service limits are not established. [Competitions](https://www.epegboard.com/features/competitions), [desktop edition](https://www.epegboard.com/featuresdesktop), [pricing](https://www.epegboard.com/pricing).

These qualifications are reflected in cells 3, 4, 5, 8, 9, 12, 13, 14 and 15. Full cells describe specific documented operations, not independently measured quality. No winner’s weakness is hidden behind a full score.

### F. When another product could be better

- **ShuttlSync:** organiser-led social clubs wanting explicit choices when courts finish at different times, live rearrangement and friendly player profiles; verify late-arrival fairness and four-court price before committing. [Official rotation](https://www.shuttlsync.com/features/fair-rotation).
- **ShuttlePilot:** clubs comfortable recording set scores and using manual courts/soft strength preferences, whose committee accepts historical sit-out balancing. [Official workflow](https://shuttlepilot.io/).
- **vsBadminton:** lightweight phone-based clubs particularly interested in attendance-normalised late-arrival credit and public overdue scores, willing to enforce new-session discipline and manually check court moves. [Selection rule](https://www.vsbadminton.co.uk/selection/), [session warning](https://www.vsbadminton.co.uk/sessions/).
- **ShuttleOps:** clubs where bookings, dues and membership collection are major administrative burdens. A 26-attendee/larger-roster club should budget against Pro’s allowance, not Standard’s 25-member ceiling, and include both Direct Debit fee components. [Pricing](https://www.shuttleops.com/pricing), [first-session payment workflow](https://www.shuttleops.com/guides/first-club-session).
- **SportyHQ or ServeLeague:** clubs/facilities with formal memberships, draws and leagues as the main job. Their richer administration does not establish a superior live next-four selector. [SportyHQ tournaments](https://sportyhq.com/features/tournament-management), [ServeLeague leagues](https://serveleague.com/features/league-management/).
- **Racket Social:** free, local-device social doubles with offline PWA operation and read-only sharing, when device-bound storage and an upper turnout beyond its typical well-tested 24 are acceptable. [Official FAQ](https://racketsocial.app/).
- **BadBoard:** Windows-based clubs preferring time-boxed rotations and accepting mode-limited reports/manual grades. [Operating modes](https://badboard.net/howItWorks.aspx), [report restrictions](https://badboard.net/extraFeatures.aspx).
- **SportRevive:** stable-attendance clubs that prefer a printed whole-night fixture sheet and end-night settlement over ongoing free-court allocation. [Badminton fixture generator](https://sportrevive.com/badminton-fixture-generator).
- **Court Buddy:** a pickleball/DUPR club rather than a badminton club, subject to its incomplete public balancing/score detail. Sport fit is reported separately and did not alter its score. [Club tools](https://court-buddy.com/clubs).
- **UBR:** players/clubs primarily seeking expectation-based badminton ratings and structured rated mixers; flexible Open Play is explicitly future functionality. [Official events](https://www.universalbadmintonrating.com/home/events).

### C and G. What the ranking can carry

Confidence is **moderate in the documentary shortlist, low in comparative on-court performance**. This is a ranking of verified evidence against the rubric, not a finding that lower-ranked products lack U features. Thirty-two canonical products were readable; ShuttleVantage Pro was not. Missing public method/edge cases cap many scores at 1 rather than prove a poor algorithm.


The leader’s verified margin over ShuttlSync is **8.5 points**. Shared resolved-cell differences are C6: +4, C9: -2.5, C10: +2.5, C11: +2.5; the runner has 4 unresolved points. The extra resolved competition evidence is not evidence that the runner lacks competitions.

**None of the leader’s margin is backed by a head-to-head performance demonstration.** The entire margin is supported by differences in documented operations, qualifications and resolved coverage. It cannot be separated into a numerical “real quality” share versus “documentation depth” share from this research. The detailed reasons/kiosk/report procedures support capability evidence more strongly; they do not prove lower latency, better balance or shorter actual waits.

Unknowns can change positions: compare each possible range, especially products with many U cells. A large upper endpoint is uncertainty, not a reason to recommend an unreadable product. The unreachable candidate’s 0 verified/100 unresolved is **not a zero capability score**. Sport is descriptive and makes no contribution to any total.

A concrete validation night should use 26 players/four courts, a deliberately uneven skill distribution, a mid-night arrival, an early departure, a voluntary rest/rejoin, two courts finishing together, a manual swap and a connection loss. Record maximum/current wait relative to time present, repeated partners/opponents, pair-strength gaps, organiser actions and recovery. This research does not claim those tests passed. The recommendation is to run that reversible pilot before replacing the club’s current method.
