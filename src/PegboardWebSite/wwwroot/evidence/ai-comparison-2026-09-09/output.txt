### Key evidence behind the leading scores

**ePegboard** has unusually detailed operational documentation rather than just feature claims. Its picker explicitly considers expected closeness, skill balance, wait fairness, recent repeats, partner/opponent variety, queue fairness and straight-back-on behaviour. It also exposes _why_ a game was selected and lets the organiser swap, lock, remove or manually construct players. ePegboard Docs+1

It documents arrival handling including regulars, drop-ins and last-minute changes, live boards, attendance, game history, ratings and payments. ePegboard+1 Its kiosk mode has explicit organiser controls and a PIN, while the Windows desktop version is fully offline. ePegboard Docs+1

The significant qualification is payments: ePegboard explicitly says its Payments feature is **a payment record, not a card machine**; the club collects cash, cheque or bank transfer and records the amount. That is why C12 is **1P, not 2F**. ePegboard Docs

Its competition workflow is also genuinely documented: balanced pairs, groups, round-robin, live standings and knockout stages, including score correction behaviour. ePegboard Docs

**QourtX** documents check-in, guest handling, live status, automatic court assignment, wait-time estimates, fair rotation, ratings/fatigue/history-based matchmaking, match history, member management, analytics and Stripe billing. Qourtx+1 It also documents live wallboards and kiosk operation. Qourtx Tournaments/ladders and payments are explicitly offered. Qourtx

The deductions are deliberate: partner rotation is documented, but not the same depth of opponent-variety rule as ePegboard; no public operational explanation facility comparable to ePegboard's "why was this game suggested?" is documented; manual override is not described with comparable precision; and offline operation is not documented. Hence 1P rather than 2F in those areas.

**ShuttlePilot** has exceptionally strong club-night documentation. It explicitly describes late arrivals, early departures, manually fixed courts, ELO-balanced sides, repeated-pair avoidance, sit-out selection across sessions, pairing constraints, score entry and phone/laptop operation. ShuttlePilot+1 It even documents the important edge case that late arrivals can be added without disturbing already-playing matches. ShuttlePilot

Its unresolved weaknesses are outside that core: attendance reporting, competition workflows and full payment/member administration are not sufficiently documented, so they are not silently scored zero.

**Racket Social** documents automatic doubles distribution, different partners/opponents, equalised sit-outs, optional skill-balanced courts, OpenSkill/Elo, live shared sessions and offline PWA operation. Racket Social+1 Its changelog gives unusually concrete evidence of the implementation, including automatic sitting-out rotation, skill-balanced matchmaking, session statistics, score logging and durable offline synchronisation. Racket Social Its tournament functionality is still described as beta, and I found no sufficient evidence for payments/member administration, hence the U values.

**ShuttleFlow** has detailed rules for its ranking system and queue operation, including skill, recency and wait-time matchmaking, manual assignment, match history, fee summaries and an explicit offline mode for Queue Masters. ShuttleFlow+1 Its ranking rules are particularly transparent, including season resets, skill tiers and anti-farming conditions. ShuttleFlow

**PaQueueKa** documents live queueing, wait-time and court-utilisation analytics, player statistics, fees/payments, skill-aware rankings and join codes. PaQueueKa+1 Its tournament management is explicitly labelled **"Coming soon"**, so that criterion is **0A**, not U. PaQueueKa

**eBadders** is one of the more substantial older systems. Its own help material describes automatic game selection, fair game counts, skill learning, variety, manual modification and player preferences. eBadders Its privacy documentation confirms that session scores, partners/opponents, game selection and payment records are actually part of the product, and that it supports tournament and league forms. eBadders

**BadBoard** is unusually strong for a legacy desktop pegboard: it explicitly supports arrival/removal, timer- or game-based operation, grades players 1–10, queued selections, manual selection, best-match suggestions and detailed statistics. BadBoard Its major practical weakness is the requirement for a Windows laptop/PC and its relatively old product documentation.

**vsBadminton** has very good operational documentation for wait-time fairness and dynamic court selection. It measures waiting time, supports players joining later, configurable court counts, pick-ahead, manual/automatic game selection and proper doubles/mixed-doubles selection. VS Badminton

# E. Material weaknesses of the winner

**ePegboard is not perfect.**

1. **Payments are bookkeeping, not payment processing.** The product explicitly says it does not take card or online payments. The club still collects cash, cheque or bank transfer and records the result. ePegboard Docs — This is the main reason the winner receives **1P rather than 2F for criterion 12**.
2. **The web version depends on connectivity.** There is an offline Windows desktop application, but that is a separate installation and costs £60/year; the web app is the newer feature-leading version. ePegboard+1
3. **The strongest operational evidence is vendor documentation, not an independent demonstration.** I have treated it as _documented_, not independently demonstrated. That distinction matters particularly for the picker and fairness algorithm.
4. **The richness can be more than a small social club needs.** A 20-player club running four courts may not need league teams, handicap tournaments, coaching workflows, inventories and extensive reports. Those capabilities nevertheless did not earn points outside their criteria.
5. **Ratings need sensible starting data.** ePegboard itself says approximate starting ratings improve the first sessions, while the engine learns new players over their early games. ePegboard Docs

# F. When another product would be better

- **Choose QourtX** if the club places unusually high value on an integrated member-facing app, payments, wallboard/kiosk and broader racquet-club administration. Its documented feature set is particularly broad. Qourtx+1
- **Choose ShuttlePilot** if the committee's overriding requirement is the live four-court social night and it is comfortable with a comparatively lightweight administrative layer. Its late-arrival, early-leaver and sit-out handling is exceptionally well specified. ShuttlePilot+1
- **Choose Racket Social** if offline PWA operation, zero cost, simple player participation and fair sit-out rotation matter more than club administration. Racket Social
- **Choose ShuttleFlow** if transparent rankings, offline Queue-Master operation and explicit anti-gaming ranking rules are priorities. ShuttleFlow
- **Choose PaQueueKa** if a simple queue/analytics/payment workflow is preferred and the forthcoming tournament functionality is not currently required. PaQueueKa+1

- **Choose vsBadminton** if the club wants very explicit wait-time-based game selection and is comfortable with a more traditional, less integrated product. VS Badminton

# G. Confidence and unresolved evidence

### Winner confidence: **High for documented capability; medium for real-world performance**

The leader's **97.5/100 is fully resolved**, which is unusual in this comparison. It is not a result produced by turning undocumented features into zeros: every criterion has affirmative evidence, with the one deliberate partial being payments.

The important qualification is that the evidence is vendor documentation. It demonstrates **documented behaviour**, not an independent observation of the algorithm operating with 18–26 mixed-strength players over several weeks.

### How much of the leader's margin is evidence availability?

The verified gap between ePegboard and QourtX is **10 points**.

I would attribute **no more than roughly 3–4 points of that gap to documentation advantage**. Both have substantial product documentation. The majority is supported by explicit differences in what is documented: ePegboard has deeper evidence for partner/opponent variety, pick explanations, manual-control edge cases, fairness rules and offline fallback.

That is an evaluator judgement, not a measurable statistical decomposition.

The bigger evidence problem is below second place: ShuttlePilot, Racket Social, eBadders and vsBadminton all have enough unresolved criteria that their _possible_ scores overlap materially with QourtX and, in some cases, the leader.

### What would QourtX have to do to overtake?

QourtX is fully resolved at **87.5**, so it cannot gain points merely by resolving U values. To beat 97.5 it would need either:

- several of its current partial scores to become demonstrably full — particularly partner/opponent variety, explainability, manual override and resilience/accessibility — **or**
- a real-world test to show that those capabilities behave more completely than their current documentation indicates, **or**
- the ePegboard trial to expose a material weakness worth more than the current 10-point gap.

Under the present evidence, QourtX would need roughly **12.5 additional weighted points** if its current partials alone were upgraded, which is possible in principle but not supported by the current documentation.

### Evidence I would verify before purchase

For the committee, I would insist on a live trial involving:

- 18, 20, 22 and 26 players;
- four courts;
- mixed abilities;
- two late arrivals after round 1;
- two early departures;
- one player temporarily sitting out;
- one manually requested pairing;
- deliberately uneven initial ratings;
- a player who has already sat out more than another;
- score entry from a shared tablet;
- loss of Wi-Fi;
- an organiser who is also playing.

That would resolve the remaining practical question much better than another marketing-page comparison.
