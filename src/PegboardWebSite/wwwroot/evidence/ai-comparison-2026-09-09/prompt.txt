SCORING DISCIPLINE
Apply these to every candidate equally, including any product whose documentation
is extensive.

a. A marketing claim is not a 2. Award 2 only where the source describes how the
   capability behaves — the rule, the edge cases, the limits. A feature bullet or a
   benefit statement without operational detail is 1.

b. Score the criterion, not the product's overall impression. A capability that meets
   part of the criterion's definition is 1, however well executed that part is.

c. Self-consistency gate. Before reporting, list every weakness or qualification you
   intend to state in section E, and check it against that product's scores. If you
   would qualify a capability in prose, it is 1, not 2. Resolve every contradiction
   in favour of the lower score, and report which scores this changed.

d. A perfect or near-perfect total is a warning sign, not a result. If any product
   scores 2 on every criterion, re-examine it before reporting: in practice that
   usually means the rubric was resolved entirely from that vendor's own material
   rather than that the product has no limitations. State explicitly what you
   re-examined and what you changed.

e. Distinguish documented from demonstrated. Where a capability is evidenced only by
   the vendor describing it, say so — for the leader as rigorously as for the rest.

f. Cross-check the standard. Identify any criterion where two products with materially
   similar evidence received different scores, and either justify the difference or
   correct it.

If material unresolved U values prevent a fair total, state that a defensible
overall ranking cannot yet be calculated and identify what must be verified.
